Anthropic published its position on open-weights models — AI whose trained parameters anyone can download and run — after being accused of wanting them banned to protect its business.

Its answer: not a ban. Instead — no powerful chips sold to China, a crackdown on industrial-scale distillation (copying a model by training on its outputs), and mandatory safety testing for every sufficiently capable model, open or closed.

"Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models."

## Telegram — Run 20:00

China's Commerce Ministry gave Beijing's first response to the US accusation that Moonshot AI copied American models to build Kimi K3.

It rejected the "distillation" claim — training a model on another's outputs to mimic it — as evidence-free, said US firms themselves call the technique standard industry practice, and vowed to "take all measures necessary" to defend its interests.

"These actions lack any actual evidence, have no legal backing, and effectively amount to a double standard."
